# PaperTrail Policy Brief — 2026-09-09

**Policy Brief – Exam‑Paper Leak Incidents in India (2004‑2026)**  

---

### 1. Overview  
- **Total incidents tracked:** 131 (2004‑2026).  
- **Trend:** Incidents were sporadic early on (1‑2 per year up to 2010). A clear rise began in 2011, peaking at 26 reported leaks in 2026. The upward trajectory coincides with broader digital diffusion and heightened media scrutiny.

### 3. Leak Mechanisms  
- **Unspecified:** 70 incidents (≈ 53 %).  
- **Social Media/WhatsApp circulation:** 18 (≈ 14 %).  
- **Insider leak:** 16 (≈ 12 %).  
- **Impersonation/Solver:** 15 (≈ 11 %).  
- **Printing/Press leak:** 7 (≈ 5 %).  
- **Tech‑enabled (device/OMR):** 5 (≈ 4 %).  

The dominance of “unspecified” reflects limited investigative detail in many reports.

- National Testing Agency (NTA) – 6 incidents

National bodies (CBSE, NTA) and the Madhya Pradesh Vyapam system dominate the sample.

### 5. Scale of Impact (Aspirants Affected)  
1. **UP Police Constable Recruitment Exam 2024** – ~4.8 million aspirants.  
2. **NEET‑UG 2024** – ~2.3 million aspirants (All‑India).  
3. **NEET‑UG 2026** – ~2.27 million aspirants (All‑India).  

These three alone represent > 9 million candidates, underscoring the systemic stakes of paper leaks.

### 6. UPA vs. NDA Era Comparison  
| Era | Years covered | Total incidents | Avg. incidents / year |
|-----|---------------|----------------|----------------------|
| UPA (2004‑May 2014) | 10 | 24 | **2.4** |
| NDA (May 2014‑now) | 13 | 107 | **8.23** |

The NDA period shows more than three‑fold higher annual incidence. Part of this rise may stem from increased media attention, better reporting mechanisms, and expanded digital channels for leak distribution, not solely from a higher underlying frequency of illicit activity.

### 7. Policy Impact – The 2024 Public Examinations Act  
- **Incidents:** 94 before the Act (pre‑June 2024) vs. 37 after (post‑June 2024).  
- **Arrests rate:** 55 % before vs. 30 % after.  
- **Confirmed‑leak rate:** 87 % before vs. 38 % after.  

*Interpretation note:* The post‑Act window spans roughly 2 years, compared with ~20 years pre‑Act. The smaller sample size limits statistical confidence; the observed declines should not be taken as definitive proof of causality.

### 8. Outlook (2027‑2028)  
- **2027 forecast:** ≈ 14.7 incidents.  
- **2028 forecast:** ≈ 16.5 incidents.  

These are directional estimates based on recent trends; actual numbers will depend on enforcement intensity, technological safeguards, and reporting practices.

---

**Bottom line:** Exam‑paper leaks have escalated in frequency and scale, concentrated in a handful of states and often tied to national exam bodies. While the 2024 Public Examinations Act coincides with a short‑term dip in reported incidents, the limited post‑implementation window warrants cautious interpretation. Continued monitoring, clearer attribution of leak mechanisms, and robust digital security measures are essential to curb the growing risk to millions of aspirants.
