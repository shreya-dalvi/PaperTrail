# PaperTrail Policy Brief — 2026-07-27

**Overview**
A total of 112 exam paper leak incidents have been tracked in India. The overall trend shows a notable increase in incidents over the years, with a significant spike in 2021 and 2022. The yearly counts reveal a fluctuating pattern, with the most recent years (2021-2026) accounting for a substantial portion of the total incidents.

**State-wise Hotspots**
The top states affected by exam paper leaks are Madhya Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh, and Rajasthan, with 13, 12, and 11 incidents, respectively. Other states, such as Uttarakhand, Bihar, and Haryana, have also experienced a significant number of leaks.

**Leak Mechanisms**
The breakdown of leak mechanisms reveals that 55 incidents are unspecified due to limited source detail. Among the specified cases, insider leaks (16), impersonation/solver (15), and social media/WhatsApp circulation (14) are the most common methods. Printing/press leaks and tech-enabled leaks account for 7 and 5 incidents, respectively.

**Conducting Bodies**
The top conducting bodies associated with exam paper leaks are Vyapam (MPPEB), CBSE, and the Uttarakhand Subordinate Service Selection Commission (UKSSSC), each with 6, 5, and 4 incidents, respectively.

**Scale of Impact**
The top incidents by aspirants affected are the UP Police Constable Recruitment Exam 2024 (4,800,000), NEET-UG 2024 (2,300,000), and NEET-UG 2026 (2,270,000). These incidents demonstrate the significant impact of exam paper leaks on a large number of students.

**UPA vs NDA Era Comparison**
A comparison of the UPA (2004-2014) and NDA (2014-present) eras reveals an average of 2.4 incidents per year during the UPA era and 6.77 incidents per year during the NDA era. However, it is essential to note that rising media and reporting attention over time may also contribute to the perceived increase in incidents.

**Policy Impact: The 2024 Public Examinations Act**
Before the Act, there were 93 incidents, whereas after the Act, there have been 19 incidents. However, it is crucial to interpret these numbers cautiously due to the small sample size of the post-Act period (~2 years vs ~20 years pre-Act). The arrests rate and confirmed rate have shown a marginal increase and decrease, respectively, after the Act.

**Outlook**
The forecast suggests a directional estimate of 7.9 incidents in 2027 and 2028. This projection should be viewed as a rough guide, considering the complexities and uncertainties surrounding exam paper leaks. As the data continues to evolve, it is essential to monitor the situation and adjust the forecast accordingly.
